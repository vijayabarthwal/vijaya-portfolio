You Are Never Ready — And That's Okay

An original personal essay about uncertainty, learning, making thoughtful choices, and growing through experience.

About This Piece

Learning Before Feeling Ready

We often wait for the right moment before doing something important. But sometimes, the right moment is simply the moment when we decide to learn.

The Essay

We often wait for the right moment before doing something important. We tell ourselves that we need more knowledge, more confidence, more experience, or simply more time. We think that one day we will wake up and finally feel ready.

But I have started to believe that this moment may never come.

A person is never completely ready for everything. There is always something more to learn, understand, practice, or improve. For me, learning is not something that should begin after we feel ready. Sometimes, learning is exactly what helps us become ready.

I experienced this myself after completing my BBA.

After graduation, I saw many people around me taking different paths. Some started jobs immediately. Some changed jobs and moved into different roles. Others prepared for government examinations or started working toward different goals. Watching all of this made me think about my own future.

At first, getting a job immediately after graduation seemed like the obvious choice. After all, starting a career early has its own advantages. But instead of simply following what everyone else was doing, I started observing the people around me and thinking about their experiences.

I asked myself questions.

What was it like for someone who started working immediately after graduation? How was their experience different from someone who continued studying? What skills were they developing? What did they enjoy about their work? What challenges did they face? And most importantly, what kind of path would help me grow?

Eventually, I decided to pursue an MBA.

It wasn't because I thought an MBA would magically guarantee me a better job. It was because I felt there was still more for me to learn.

I wanted more knowledge, more exposure, and the opportunity to develop skills while learning from professors and the people around me. I wanted to understand not only what I wanted to do, but also why I wanted to do it.

That experience changed the way I think about being “ready.”

I realized that I wasn't necessarily feeling ready to enter the next stage of my career. But I was ready to learn.

Sometimes, learning is exactly what helps us become ready.

And sometimes, I think opportunities are a little like a piece of cake.

Imagine seeing a piece of cake in front of you. You can keep looking at it and wondering whether you should taste it. You can wait for the perfect moment or worry about whether you will like it. Or you can simply take a bite.

Maybe you will love it. Maybe you won't.

But after taking that bite, you will know something you didn't know before.

I think learning works in much the same way. We can spend a lot of time waiting until we feel completely prepared, but opportunities rarely arrive with a guarantee that everything will work out perfectly. Sometimes, we have to try, experience something, learn from it, and then decide what comes next.

This doesn't mean we should make every decision without thinking. My decision to pursue an MBA came from observing, asking questions, comparing experiences, and thinking carefully about what I wanted to learn.

For me, that is the difference between blindly following a path and consciously choosing one.

I am still learning. I don't think that will ever stop.

There will always be a new skill to develop, a new idea to understand, a new challenge to face, or something I can do better than I did yesterday.

And maybe that is the point.

It wasn't about being ready to do something. It was about being ready to learn something.

Because we may never feel completely ready.

But we can always be ready to learn.
